# New evidence narrows the trading shortlist

The new evidence leaves a short list. **Five rules deserve forward paper testing**: gold 50-day momentum with ATR exits, gold long in Asian hours only, bitcoin held above its 50-day average, and, at lower priority, the ETH Donchian ensemble and ETH "MAX10". **No US-oil rule makes the list.** The founding papers behind these rules hold up where they could be checked. Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen report trend following profitable net of fees for over a century. Faber's 10-month average rule keeps working when its settings are changed. Gao and co-authors' first-half-hour effect is real in their sample. Zarattini's crypto breakout ensemble reports a **bitcoin-only 30% net annual growth at a Sharpe ratio of 1.56**. But no primary text could be opened in this round, and none of these papers has been independently replicated after costs on 2025–2026 data. Live 2025–2026 markets gave mixed verdicts. Slow gold trend rules held through the January 2026 peak and exited only around June, about 20% lower. Diversified trend funds made money in 2026 mainly from bonds and oil, not gold. Oil intraday momentum looks fragile everywhere it has been retested: market shocks destroy it, futures friction swallows it, and in the project's own Exness ticks the gross signal was about zero against a spread of about 2.6 basis points. Two cost findings matter most. First, Exness's median gold spread works out to about 0.6 basis points per round trip. That is close to the very low cost the Forecast-to-Fill paper assumed, so the earlier worry that retail spreads would erase that rule was overstated. Second, the gold Asian-session test probably pays no overnight swap at all, because it opens after the daily rollover and closes before the next one. The rule that does pay swap is the multi-day gold momentum rule, unless the account's default "extended swap-free" status covers gold.

## The four founding papers survive only at the abstract level

**None of the four primary texts could be read this round.** The network refused AQR, Faber's site, SSRN mirrors, Concretum and the archive hosts. What follows rests on abstracts, citing papers and secondary summaries, and the gaps are flagged.

**Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen, "A Century of Evidence on Trend-Following Investing"** is the backbone for slow trend in commodities. Secondary sources agree on the scope: **67 markets in commodities, equities, bonds and currencies, 1880–2016, with no losing decade**. They report **gross annual returns of 18.0% and 11.0% net of fees, at 9.7% volatility** ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/trend-following-is-everywhere/)). Another summary gives **7.3% a year above cash and a net Sharpe of 0.76** ([Investing Canvas](https://investingcanvas.substack.com/p/tales-from-the-underground-137-years)). The two fit together if cash averaged about 3.7% a year (11.0% minus 7.3%), since 7.3/9.7 ≈ 0.75. That reconciliation is arithmetic, not a reported figure. Two facts temper the century-long number. An AQR document projects a forward-looking **Sharpe of only about 0.4 net of fees and costs** ([Efficient Capital / AQR](https://www.efficient.com/resources/a-century-of-evidence-on-trend-following-investing)). The early working-paper versions also start in 1903 rather than 1880, so figures from different versions are not comparable ([CBS](https://research.cbs.dk/en/publications/a-century-of-evidence-on-trend-following-investing/)). A 2025 paper describes the main finding as follows: **twelve-month momentum gives "strong and stable returns" over the century, while shorter implementations "tend to be noisier and less persistent"** ([Etienne et al., arXiv 2510.23150](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.23150)). The often-quoted construction is an equal blend of 1-, 3- and 12-month signals, each sized to constant volatility and rebalanced monthly. Its fee model and any gold- or crude-specific results remain **unverified**. The paper therefore supports "slow trend works across many markets". It does not support any single-market crude or gold number.

**Faber, "A Quantitative Approach to Tactical Asset Allocation"** contributes the simplest rule in the set. At each month-end, **hold an asset if its monthly close is above its 10-month simple moving average; otherwise hold cash**. According to summaries, returns below the average are about 60% lower and volatility about 30% higher ([the7circles](https://the7circles.uk/tactical-asset-allocation-meb-faber/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228202718_A_Quantitative_Approach_to_Tactical_Asset_Allocation)). The 2013 update added 2008–2012. A blog summary of that update reports **volatility of 7.3%, a maximum drawdown of 9.4% and a Sharpe of about 0.6** for 2006–2012. That is mostly a story of avoiding 2008, not of extra return ([the7circles](https://the7circles.uk/tactical-asset-allocation-meb-faber/)). An independent recheck found that **10 months is not the best setting on any measure, but results are stable across neighbouring lengths**. That is the right kind of robustness ([L. Scott, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/quantitative-approach-tactical-asset-allocation-revisited-louis-scott)). The 2018 "10 years later" figures were not found. Faber publishes **no gold-only or oil-only results**, so his rule enters this report only as a generic slow filter.

**Gao, Han, Li and Zhou, "Market intraday momentum"** (*Journal of Financial Economics*, 2018) is confirmed at the abstract level. Using S&P 500 ETF (SPY) data from 1993 to 2013, they show that **the first half-hour return, measured from the previous day's close, predicts the last half-hour return**. The effect is stronger on volatile, high-volume, recession and major-news days ([SSRN 2440866](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2440866)). Note the phrase "measured from the previous day's close": the predictor includes the overnight move. The paper's out-of-sample fit, its timing-strategy returns and its cost analysis could not be read. Follow-up studies split sharply.

On crude, Wen and co-authors find the USO oil fund's first half-hour predicts its last half-hour "both IS and OS" over 2006–2018. They **attribute the predictability to the overnight component** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0264999319310417)). Chinese crude futures show reversal instead ([Economic Modelling 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0264999321000134)). In Chinese gold futures, the effect **disappeared once night trading began** ([J. Int. Fin. Markets 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1044028325000110)). The most direct stress test is a May 2026 falsification study. It tried 14 intraday signal families on micro Nasdaq futures over 2021–2025 with walk-forward out-of-sample years, and **none passed**. Eleven had gross edges of 0.07–1.50 index points per trade, below the 2.0-point round-trip friction. The closest near-miss was a first-30-minute rule inspired by Wen et al. It made only 35 out-of-sample trades and lost in 2024 ([Mesfin, arXiv 2605.04004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04004)). That study did not test Gao's exact sign rule, but it shows how little room costs leave.

**Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon, "Catching Crypto Trends"** (Swiss Finance Institute RP 25-80, revised October 2025) remains the only crypto trend study that reports results after fees ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2580.html)). The rule is a long-only ensemble of nine breakout systems with lookbacks of 5, 10, 20, 30, 60, 90, 150, 250 and 360 days. Each system enters when the close rises above its lookback's highest close. It exits on a trailing stop that ratchets up to the midpoint of the lookback's highest and lowest close. The nine are averaged and sized to **25% target volatility on 90-day realised volatility, with leverage capped at 200%**. The new piece of evidence is a bitcoin-only result from the authors' firm: **from January 2015, a net-of-fee CAGR of 30% and a Sharpe of 1.56** ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/catching-crypto-trends-a-tactical-approach-for-bitcoin-and-altcoins/)). The rotating top-20-coin basket reports **a Sharpe above 1.5 and 10.8% annual alpha over bitcoin** ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2580.html)). An earlier paraphrase put it at about 18% net CAGR at 0.10% costs ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/crypto-asset-trend-following-strategies/)). So the basket's benefit is diversification and alpha, not a higher Sharpe, and bitcoin alone grows faster because volatility targeting levers it differently. Results at the paper's 0.25% and 0.50% cost levels, ETH-only results, drawdowns and the authors' cost-mitigation method all remain **unseen**.

## Gold's 2026 crash gave the trend rules a live test

### Slow rules exited about 20% below the top

No independent academic replication was found for either main gold study. Baur and co-authors' 24-month momentum and 36/48-month moving-average rules still rest on one author team. They acknowledge that their outperformance "may simply result from chance", which is why they used a data-snooping correction that the rules only narrowly missed ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/fundamental-valuation/gold-timing-strategies/)). The Forecast-to-Fill preprint has drawn **no critique or replication as of 11 November 2026**. Only listings and an AI summary aggregator turn up ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08571v1)). Its own ablation says performance "collapses" without the trend and momentum parts ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08571v1)).

The live market supplied what the literature did not. Gold peaked in late January 2026: **spot about $5,589–$5,608 on 28 January and COMEX $5,626.80 on 29 January**. It then fell 8–12% within days ([StoneX](https://stonexbullion.com/en/blog/what-was-the-highest-price-for-gold/); [EBC](https://www.ebc.com/forex/gold-highest-price-ever-xauusd-record-2026)). GLD was **25.1% below its peak by 23 July** ([ChartRow](https://chartrow.com/gold/drawdown)). Trend funds flipped **net short gold on 19 March 2026, the first time in two years**, about seven weeks after the top ([Kpler](https://www.kpler.com/blog/ctas-capitalise-on-energy-trends-in-turbulent-q1)). The slower rules were later. Gold first closed below its **200-day average (about $4,415) on about 9 June 2026**, its first close below that line since October 2023 ([Saxo](https://www.home.saxo/content/articles/commodities/gold-slips-below-200-day-average-as-inflation-jobs-and-fed-risks-bite-09062026)). It ended June below its **10-month average (about $4,453)** ([Mezha](https://mezha.net/eng/bukvy/53a3009b_gold_faces_downward/)). Commentary noted that gold had "entered a bear market while still holding above the 200-day" ([Barchart via Observer-Reporter](https://stocks.observer-reporter.com/observerreporter/article/barchart-2026-4-1-golds-200-day-bounce-reversal-signal-or-market-trap)). By October it was about **10% below its 200-day average** ([WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-market-commentary-october-2026)).

So the Faber-speed rules did what trend filters do: they **missed the top by roughly 20% and saved only the second half of the fall**. The 24-month and 36/48-month rules were probably still long throughout. Two years earlier gold was far lower, so their signals never turned. That is an inference from price levels and has not been computed. The project's screen agrees with the general profile. Its 200-day rule made **+6.6% against +1.1% for buy-and-hold, with a −22.1% maximum drawdown against −27.0%**, but on only two trades ([shortlist screen](../research/shortlist_2025/README.md)).

### Trend funds earned their 2026 gains outside gold

Diversified trend indices tell a different story from gold-only rules. The SG Trend Index made only **+2.39% in 2025**, a year described as "modest gains with significant intra-year volatility" ([Top Traders Unplugged](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-january-2025-2/)). 2026 started well: **+4.71% in January**, and **+2.85% in April, with +24.37% over the trailing 12 months** ([TTU April 2026](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-april-2026/)). March, by contrast, saw "every benchmark finishing negative" as gold fell **9.6% in one week** ([AlphaMaven](https://alpha-maven.com/story/managed-futures/march-2026-when-convexity-cuts-both-ways-march-ended-trend-followings-two-month-winning-streak-with-every-benchmark-finishing-negative-a-sustained-three-week-repricing-of-precious-metals-defined-the-month-silver-crashed-144-mid-march-gold-fell-96)). Trend hedge funds were up **15.7% through Q3 2026** ([HedgeCo](https://hedgeco.net/news/10/2026/trend-following-hedge-funds-returned-15-7-through-the-third-quarter-outpacing-the-sp-500.html)). Those gains were credited to **"betting early against bonds and on oil"**, not to gold ([political.org](https://political.org/2026/10/05/quant-hedge-funds-outpace-sp-500-in-2026-by-betting-early-against-bonds-and-on-oil/)). One live fund did profit from gold. AQR's Australian managed-futures fund returned **28.0% net in the year to February 2026, with long gold futures among its top positions at 4.4%** ([AQR Australia](https://australia.aqr.com/-/media/Australia-Documents/Basic-Information/Monthly-Report/MFFAUD.pdf?sc_lang=en)). The lesson: trend following as a portfolio worked in 2025–2026, but that says little about any single gold rule.

### The session split widened, and the screen used clean prices

The overnight pattern has new support, though it is descriptive. The World Gold Council reported gold **+12.97% during Asian hours, −15.08% during US hours and −1.32% during European hours in 2026 up to 26 June**. It linked the split to Asian ETF buying and Western ETF selling, including a record outflow of about $12bn from North American gold ETFs in March 2026 ([FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/a-strange-dichotomy-gold-up-on-the-year-in-asian-markets-down-big-in-the-west-202607131959); [WGC](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-mid-year-outlook-2026-point-break)). That is a flow regime, and flow regimes reverse. No 2023–2026 peer-reviewed test of an Asian-long, US-flat gold rule after costs exists.

There is also a warning about data quality. Quantpedia showed that a famous gold-miner ETF "overnight drift" largely came from unreliable opening prints in daily data. Using one-minute data with execution at 9:31 made the anomaly "diminish significantly" ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/dangers-of-relying-on-ohlc-prices-the-case-of-overnight-drift-in-gdx-etf/)). The project's screen is immune to that artefact, because it filled every trade on real Exness bid/ask ticks. Its result, **+20.0% for Asian hours (22:00–07:00 UTC) against +1.3% for US hours (13:00–21:00 UTC)**, matches the WGC split in direction. Per trade, the Asian rule made **4.26 basis points net of spread at 1× costs and 3.66 at 2×**, with 54% winners over 472 trades. (A basis point, bp, is 0.01%.) The two cost runs imply a real round-trip spread of about 0.6 bp. That is the project's own arithmetic from the [screen results](../research/shortlist_2025/results/results.json).

### Gold's real spread is far tighter than the earlier report assumed

The earlier report dismissed Forecast-to-Fill partly because its baseline of **0.7 bp per round trip** looked impossible for a retail trader. The screen's tick data contradicts that. Exness XAUUSDm's **median spread was $0.16 an ounce**, roughly 0.3–0.6 bp across 2025–2026 price levels, consistent with the 0.6 bp implied above ([screen results](../research/shortlist_2025/results/results.json)). Spreads widen around news and the daily break, so medians flatter the real cost. Still, the paper's own cost-stress collapse (Sharpe about 0 at 1.5× costs) was a property of its higher-turnover full rule. The screen's simplified version made only **17 trades** and returned **+49.1% at 1× costs and +49.0% at 2×, against +32.0% for buy-and-hold, with a −13.4% drawdown against −27.0%** ([shortlist screen](../research/shortlist_2025/README.md)). That simplified version entered when the close was above the close 50 days ago and the 20-day average was higher than 5 days earlier. It exited at −2×ATR14 from entry, at −1.5×ATR14 from the peak, or after 30 days. An independent re-implementation matched it within 0.19 points ([verification log](../research/shortlist_2025/VERIFICATION.md)).

## US oil's intraday edge lost to a 2.6 basis-point spread

The oil-specific evidence weakened on every front. The USO intraday result has **never been independently replicated after costs on post-2018 data**. Its best follow-ups come from the same author group. A 2023 *Energy Economics* paper extends USO data through 2022 and finds that **"most market shocks obliterate the predictive power of first half-hour returns"** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0140988323006199)). 2025–2026, with OPEC swings, war risk and oil reversals, was exactly that kind of regime. The EIA-day variant says that on inventory-release days the release half-hour, not the first, predicts the close ([SSRN 3822093](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3822093)). Its authors include Wen and Xu, so it is not independent, and no test after 2021 was found. The only practitioner retest, on USO rather than crude futures, reports either **1.85% a year** or a "19.6% annual returns" headline, depending on the page. The two cannot be reconciled ([QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/crude-oil-futures/)).

The project's screen makes the cost problem concrete. Its oil intraday rule lost **−14.8% at 1× costs and −24.4% at 2×, with 44% winners over 456 trades** ([shortlist screen](../research/shortlist_2025/README.md)). The per-trade figures were **−3.46 bp at 1× and −6.09 bp at 2×**. The gap between the two runs puts one round-trip spread at about 2.6 bp, consistent with Exness USOILm's **median $0.02 spread** ([screen results](../research/shortlist_2025/results/results.json)). Before spread, then, the signal made roughly **−0.8 bp per trade: essentially zero, slightly the wrong way**. "Wrong direction" in the README is accurate but slightly misleading. The rule did not reverse strongly. It had no edge, and the spread did the damage. That matches the futures falsification study, where gross intraday edges sat below friction.

Two specification gaps in the screen are worth fixing before anyone concludes the rule is dead in its published form. First, the screen used only the **09:30–10:00 New York return** as the predictor. Wen et al. attribute the predictability to the **overnight component**, and Gao measures the first half-hour from the previous close, so the published predictor includes the overnight move. Second, the screen used the 10:30–11:00 window every Wednesday and **did not model holiday-shifted EIA weeks**. By the calendar below, that mis-keyed about **13 of the roughly 95 Wednesdays** in the January 2025–October 2026 window, about 3% of all trades. That is too few to change the verdict, but it should be fixed. Even a corrected rule must clear a 2.6 bp spread with a signal that 2025–2026 shocks were likely to erase.

Slow crude trend gets little help either. A 2024 *Pacific-Basin Finance Journal* study found **"no supportive evidence" of time-series momentum across about 55 commodity futures** and none in the main Asian crude market. It also found that momentum does not significantly beat a naive no-predictability strategy ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X24002245)). In 2025, energy was a losing sector for trend funds. The SG Trend Index was **−3.19% in February as "crude oil lost ground"** and **−7.56% year to date in August with "reversals in energy"** ([TTU Feb 2025](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-february-2025/); [TTU Aug 2025](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-august-2025/)). In 2026 the sign flipped: oil longs drove gains, though **May 2026's losses came mainly from oil reversals** ([Wisdom Trading](https://wisdomtrading.com/insights/managed-futures-may-2026-trend-recap/)). The screen's monthly 1/3/12 blend made **+12.9% against +60.6% for buy-and-hold on three trades**, and the 55/20 Turtle rule lost 6.3% ([shortlist screen](../research/shortlist_2025/README.md)). Crude trend works as one slice of a diversified book. Alone, it has neither published support nor a live record that justifies a paper test.

## Crypto evidence stays with one team, and bitcoin alone looks weaker than the basket

Independent work on simple bitcoin moving-average rules is now clearly split. On the positive side, Detzel and co-authors find that price-to-moving-average ratios forecast daily bitcoin returns in and out of sample ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323324259_Bitcoin_Predictability_and_Profitability_via_Technical_Analysis)). On the negative side, Hudson and Urquhart tested 5–100-day averages with breakeven costs and found that bitcoin "does not offer any positive returns in the out-of-sample period", while some other coins did ([Annals of Operations Research](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10479-019-03357-1.pdf)). A 2024 *International Review of Economics & Finance* study likewise "does not find out-of-sample outperformance for bitcoin" ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1059056024003010)). A Monash study of BTC, ETH and crypto indexes found high Sharpe ratios for short lookbacks, but warned that frequent rebalancing eats them ([Monash CFS](https://www.monash.edu/business/monash-centre-for-financial-studies/our-research/all-projects/investment-strategy/trend-following-strategies-for-crypto-investors)). Grayscale's 50-day study was last updated in August 2023 ([Grayscale](https://research.grayscale.com/reports/the-trend-is-your-friend-managing-bitcoins-volatility-with-momentum-signals)).

The screen's **BTC close-above-SMA50 result of +28.6% at 1× and +23.5% at 2×, against −21.0% buy-and-hold over 20 trades**, fits the one thing trend filters reliably do: avoid much of a falling market. It does not resolve the out-of-sample dispute ([shortlist screen](../research/shortlist_2025/README.md)). The ETH version made +151.2%, but on only nine trades.

MAX-N has no independent test at all. The rule is: if today's close is the highest of the last N days, hold the next day. Quantpedia's own revisit, February 2022 to August 2024, says MAX "remains very effective", with no fee adjustment shown and no update since ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/revisiting-trend-following-and-mean-reversion-strategies-in-bitcoin/)). The screen's split fits the academic pattern that altcoins show predictability where bitcoin does not. **ETH MAX10 made +37.8% at 1× and +24.9% at 2×; BTC MAX10 lost −6.7% and −16.9%.** But ETH's ordering (MAX10 above MAX20) is the reverse of Quantpedia's bitcoin finding that MAX20 beat MAX10. That inconsistency is a reason for caution, not a refinement to adopt.

The Zarattini ensemble faces a comparability problem in the screen. The user forbids leverage, so the screen capped exposure at 1.0 instead of the paper's 2.0. Because crypto's volatility runs well above the 25% target, average exposure fell to **only 9–16%**. That produced **−6.3% on BTC and +4.2% on ETH (9% exposure)** ([shortlist screen](../research/shortlist_2025/README.md)). This is a different, much smaller bet than the one behind the 30%/1.56 bitcoin figure, so neither result confirms nor refutes the paper. The basket design also matters. The independent studies that find no bitcoin-only edge suggest that much of Zarattini's value comes from rotating across coins and from volatility targeting. A single-coin, unlevered version should expect far less.

The **21:00–23:00 UTC bitcoin seasonality** has no test after June 2023 beyond marketing restatements ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/the-seasonality-of-bitcoin/); [TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/script/IzFZxayj/)). It also sits on top of the Exness rollover. On a CFD, a position held across 21:00 UTC in summer or 22:00 UTC in winter pays swap, and pays it triple on Fridays, which Quantpedia found to be the strongest day. A July 2026 paper argues that classic bitcoin cycle indicators (MVRV, the Mayer Multiple, Pi Cycle) have stopped firing as volatility falls. Its own out-of-sample evidence is "suggestive, not decisive" ([arXiv 2607.26188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26188)). It concerns on-chain indicators, not the price rules here.

## Exness swap rules and the EIA calendar decide the borderline rules

### Swap can be zero, small or decisive depending on account status

On Exness Standard accounts, cost is **spread plus overnight swap, with no commission**. Only Zero and Raw Spread accounts charge commission ([Exness Help](https://get.exness.help/hc/en-us/articles/360012007919-Are-trading-accounts-charged-a-commission-fee)). Swap is booked at the daily rollover: **21:00 GMT in summer and 22:00 GMT in winter** ([Exness Help: About swap](https://get.exness.help/hc/en-us/articles/360014709151-About-swap)). For BTCUSD and ETHUSD, the Friday-night swap is charged triple to cover the weekend ([Exness Crypto](https://www.exness.com/crypto/)). Gold and oil each have a daily trading break around rollover, quoted as **20:58–22:02 for gold and 20:59–22:01 for oil** in server time ([Exness commodities](https://www.exness.com/commodities/)). Exness's help centre says **"Extended Swap-free" status is the default for accounts of clients in non-Islamic countries**. The standard tier covers crypto, and the extended tier adds **USOIL, UKOIL, XNGUSD and XAUUSD**. That text comes from a Zendesk mirror, not the main site ([Exness Help mirror](https://exnesshelpcenter1650503396.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/10301387770908-Are-swap-free-trading-accounts-available-in-non-Islamic-countries)). Clients registered in eligible Islamic countries fall under a separate all-instrument swap-free regime. Third parties say the status can be withdrawn for long-held positions, which official documents do not confirm ([FX Recap](https://fxrecap.com/top-broker/exness-review/islamic-account)). Exness's own USOIL page confirms USOIL is swap-free under the program ([Exness USOIL](https://www.exness.com/commodities/usoil/)).

No reliable current swap numbers were found for any of the three symbols. Third-party XAUUSD figures disagree tenfold: **about −$3.50, −$2.10 or −$42 per lot per night for longs** ([tradingzenith](https://tradingzenith-net.pages.dev/artigos/exness-gold-trading-india); [lowspreadbroker](https://lowspreadbroker-com.pages.dev/artigos/lowest-spread-broker-gold-xauusd)). Other brokers charged **−$57.55 to −$95.58 per lot per night in September 2026** ([Coinspectator](https://coinspectator.com/mainstream/2026/09/03/the-battle-for-gold-traders-moves-overnight-32-accounts-charge-one-offers-positive-swaps/)). BTCUSD long swaps are quoted at **about −$12.62 to −$17.64 per lot per night** on non-official pages ([ex-platforms](https://ex-platforms.com/en/swap-rates)). For comparison, IG charges roughly 15–23% a year on long bitcoin CFDs ([IG](https://www.ig.com/uk/help-and-support/articles/681837-cryptocurrencies-cfd-product-details)).

The Asian-session finding reverses the earlier assumption. Both the screen's README and the gold research note assumed the Asian rule pays a nightly swap. Exness's published rollover times and the screen code suggest it opens just after the swap is booked and closes before the next booking. That is an inference to verify, not a fact. If it is wrong and the full $30–$95 rate applies with a triple Wednesday, the swap would take about **20–80% of the rule's 4.3 bp per-trade edge**.

Other Exness conditions also affect the backtests. Gold leverage is **capped at 1:200 from 15 minutes before to 5 minutes after major news**. USOIL's fixed **1:1000** leverage drops to **1:20 during higher-margin periods** ([Exness Help: HMR](https://get.exness.help/hc/en-us/articles/20618327917596-Higher-Margin-Requirements-HMR)). No simulated trade should fall inside the daily break.

A forward paper test can catch implementation and cost errors within weeks: fills at the daily break, real swap entries, spread spikes at news. It cannot confirm an edge quickly. A strategy's t-statistic is roughly its Sharpe ratio times the square root of years, so a Sharpe-1 rule needs about nine years to clear t = 3. Each test should therefore be pre-registered with its exact rule, its cost accounting and an evaluation date, as the shortlist screen was.

## Conclusion

The biggest change is in which costs bind. The earlier research assumed spreads would kill the gold rules and ignored timing around the rollover. The tick data shows the reverse. Exness's gold spread is tight enough that the Forecast-to-Fill-style rule's main cost risk is the multi-day swap, not the spread. The Asian-session rule, flagged as the swap-exposed one, probably dodges swap entirely by opening just after rollover. Oil shows the opposite case: the spread is the whole cost, and the signal underneath it was gone. So the account's swap-free status and the exact rollover clock matter more to these results than any parameter choice. They should be read from the MT5 symbol specification and a demo statement before any paper test counts.

The second lesson concerns what "out of sample" meant in 2025–2026. The one large live test, gold's 25–30% fall from its January 2026 record, behaved exactly as the trend literature predicts. Medium-speed systems stepped aside within seven weeks, Faber-speed filters within about five months, and the slowest rules not at all. Diversified trend funds profited from bonds and oil while the single-market rules on those same assets had little to show. That supports a portfolio view. The surviving rules are better treated as small, cheaply monitored pieces of a wider trend book than as standalone edges. Each still awaits its first independent replication.
