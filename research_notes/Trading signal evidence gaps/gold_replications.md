## Q1. Is there any independent replication of Baur et al. (2020) gold timing rules or of Forecast-to-Fill (arXiv 2511.08571)?

### Takeaway
No independent academic replication of either study turned up. The Baur et al. result rests on the original author team (working paper 2018, IRFA 2020). Forecast-to-Fill is a single non-peer-reviewed preprint, and no critique or replication was found. Only practitioner blog posts discuss either one. The closest thing to "out-of-sample" evidence for the slow rules is how they behaved live in 2025–2026: they stayed long through the rally, then turned bearish in roughly June 2026, about five months and about 20%+ after the late-January peak (see Q4).

### Cited Findings
- The Baur study first circulated as Bartsch, Baur, Dichtl & Drobetz (2018), "Investing in the Gold Market: Market Timing or Buy-and-Hold?" (SSRN 3202658). It used monthly spot and futures data from December 1979 to December 2015, with out-of-sample tests from January 1990. It tested 4,095 seasonal, 18 technical and 15 fundamental rules. "One to three technical trading strategies, led by 24-month momentum, significantly outperform buy-and-hold, depending on test restrictiveness. No fundamental trading strategy significantly outperforms buy-and-hold." The authors attribute the edge to behavioural biases that create trends. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/fundamental-valuation/gold-timing-strategies/); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/an-extensive-test-of-market-timing-strategies-in-the-gold-market/)
- The authors acknowledge that because "all timing strategies are tested on the same data set, the observed outperformance may simply result from chance." This is why they use SPA/step-wise tests. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/fundamental-valuation/gold-timing-strategies/)
- The underlying time-series momentum evidence for gold futures is Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012), which Baur et al. cite. That is a related but earlier and broader result, not a replication. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/fundamental-valuation/gold-timing-strategies/)
- Forecast-to-Fill was submitted on 11 November 2025 (q-fin.TR) by Mainak Singha, Jose Aguilera-Toste and Vinayak Lahiri. It claims β≈0.03 to gold, a bootstrap Sharpe CI of [2.49, 3.27], an SPA p-value of 0.000, and capacity of about 0.07% of daily CME gold volume (a "positive-growth frontier" up to about $1bn AUM). EconPapers lists three authors, while IDEAS lists two. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08571v1); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08571v1); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/arxpapers/2511.08571.htm); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2511.08571.html)
- Its walk-forward design trains on a 10-year lookback and tests the next 6 months (OOS 2015–2025). It reports a maximum drawdown of 0.52% net of a 0.7 bp linear cost plus square-root impact. Its own ablation says performance "collapses" when trend or momentum components are removed or the signal is flipped. The abstract lists the same 43% for "annualized return" and "CAGR", which suggests a labelling problem. That 43% is the vol-scaled figure the prior report already flagged. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08571); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08571v1)
- Searches for "Forecast-to-Fill" replications return only the arXiv/RePEc listings, an AI paper-summary aggregator and unrelated FXStreet pages. No critique, comment or replication was found as of 2026-11-11. — [search results incl. arxiv.deeppaper.ai listing](https://arxiv.deeppaper.ai/papers/2511.08571v1)
- Practitioner-only material on similar gold trend rules: Unger Academy blog posts on two trend strategies "that capitalized on the 2025 rally" (Supertrend / high-low breakout). These are vendor education marketing with no independent costs or OOS design. — [Unger Academy](https://ungeracademy.com/?p=8864)

### Inferences
- The Baur et al. family remains a single-team, borderline-significance result. Its "replication" in 2025–26 is a live record of essentially one or two signal changes, which cannot statistically confirm anything.
- Forecast-to-Fill has no outside scrutiny. Its own cost-stress table (from the prior report: Sharpe ≈0 at 1.5× costs) remains the most decisive evidence against using it as a retail CFD edge. The template's exit logic (2×ATR stop, 1.5×ATR trail, 30-day timeout) is, however, fully specified and reusable as a test harness.

### Gaps
- No full-text access was possible, so I could not check for any 2025–2026 citing papers on Google Scholar or SSRN that re-run MOM24 or MA36/48 on post-2015 data.
- No published live or paper-trading track record of the Forecast-to-Fill rule over Nov 2025–Nov 2026 was found. Gold's huge volatility in that period (a January 2026 spike, then 8–12% daily-scale crashes) would be a natural stress test, but nobody appears to have reported it.

## Q2. Recent (2023–2026) evidence on gold overnight vs intraday returns and Asian-session drift, and whether it survives spreads and CFD swap costs

### Takeaway
The session split was extreme in 2026, at least descriptively. The World Gold Council (via secondary coverage) reported gold **+12.97% in Asian hours vs −15.08% in US hours and −1.32% in European hours, year to date to about 26 June 2026**. That is descriptive data on a single half-year, not a tested trading rule. Quantpedia's 2025 work shows that close-to-open "overnight drift" results in gold-related ETFs are badly inflated by OHLC open-price artefacts. On retail CFDs, holding a long XAUUSD position overnight now costs roughly $10–$95 per standard lot per night depending on broker (with triple charges midweek). That is large relative to a daily session edge, and the prior report's practitioner view ("too small to trade by itself") still stands.

### Cited Findings
- WGC Mid-Year Outlook 2026 (data as of 26 June 2026), as reported secondarily: Asia sessions +12.97% YTD, US sessions −15.08%, Europe −1.32%. The WGC said "many of the pullbacks occurred during US hours" and rebounds "generally occurred during Asian hours." It credited Asian ETF inflows and the "increasingly relevant role that Asian investors (and consumers) play in price discovery." Another secondary rounding of the same figures is +12.9% vs −15%. — [FXStreet / Money Metals repost](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/a-strange-dichotomy-gold-up-on-the-year-in-asian-markets-down-big-in-the-west-202607131959); [Money Metals](https://www.moneymetals.com/news/2026/07/12/a-strange-dichotomy-gold-up-on-the-year-in-asian-markets-down-big-in-the-west-005059); primary report (not fetchable): [WGC Mid-Year Outlook 2026](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/gold-mid-year-outlook-2026-point-break)

This is a different mechanism, tied mostly to Asian and Indian demand calendars. — [Quantpedia gold tag](https://quantpedia.com/tag/gold/page/2/)
- Searches found no 2023–2026 peer-reviewed paper that tests a gold Asian-session-long / US-session-flat rule after costs.

### Inferences
- Rough cost arithmetic (my inference): one standard XAUUSD lot is 100 oz, about $400k–$500k notional at 2026 prices of $4,000–$5,000. A $30–$95 nightly swap is then about 0.6–2.4 bp per night, about 2–9% a year of carry for a permanently long position (about 365 charged nights a year, with weekends covered by the triple-swap day), plus about 2–4 bp per round trip in spread (spread figures not verified here). An Asian-long / US-flat rule that opens and closes every day pays the spread daily and still crosses the 22:00/23:00 server rollover, so it pays the swap anyway. That combination plausibly consumes a large part of the 2026 session split once it is averaged over normal years.
- The 2026 Asia vs US split is consistent with a flow story (Asian ETF and physical buying vs Western ETF liquidation). Flow regimes can reverse, so this should be tested as a hypothesis, not assumed persistent.
- CFD backtests should use the broker's actual swap history, or a funding-rate model (SOFR + markup), rather than a constant. US funding rates moved during 2025–2026, so swaps were not constant.

### Gaps
- Exact Exness XAUUSD swap long/short values and the swap-free policy could not be verified (exness.com was blocked).
- No WGC primary data was read for session returns in H2 2026 or in 2025. It is unknown whether the Asia > US pattern persisted after June 2026.
- No 2023–2026 academic test of the COMEX overnight/day-session split (an update of Blose & Gondhalekar) was found.

## Q3. How did trend-following funds and indices (SG CTA, SG Trend, BTOP50, AQR) perform, especially on gold, in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
2025 was a modest year for trend followers despite gold's huge rally: SG Trend about +2.4% and BTOP50 about +2.8%, per secondary reports. Gold was a long-side contributor, but the year was choppy. 2026 started strongly, with SG Trend +4.71% in January as gold spiked to records near $5,600 in late January. A secondary report has SG Trend at about +14.9% YTD through October 2026. The live evidence is consistent with trend following's known profile: medium-term systems got out of long gold within weeks of the top and partly monetised the fall, but metals reversals in March and June 2026 cost them.

## Q4. 2025–2026 papers and evidence on gold return predictability, and how the slow trend rules behaved live in 2026

### Takeaway
No 2025–2026 academic paper on gold return predictability (momentum, real rates or central-bank buying) was found. The material is commentary and bank or WGC research. Real rates are described as having decoupled in 2022–2025 and partly "recoupled" in 2026 as rate-hike risk returned. Live slow-trend signals: gold closed below its 200-day SMA (about $4,415) in early June 2026 for the first time since October/November 2023, briefly reclaimed it, and was about 10% below its 200-day by the WGC's October 2026 commentary. A Faber-style 10-month SMA (about $4,453) was also reported broken in June 2026. Both are about 20% below the late-January peak, so the faster slow rules exited late and gave back much of the top.

### Inferences
- Live 2026 behaviour of the "fast slow" rules (200-day, 10-month): they held through the January blow-off top and exited only around June 2026, near $4,400–$4,450, about 20% below the about $5,600 peak. They then avoided the further decline to roughly 10% below the 200-day by October. This is the textbook trend-filter profile: they miss the top and capture only the second half of a bear leg.
- For a 2025-01-01 backtest, this means the 200-day and 10-month rules probably produced one exit (around June 2026) and possibly a whipsaw re-entry around mid-June. The 24-month momentum and 36/48-month MA rules probably produced no exit at all. This is a reasoned estimate from price levels and should be verified with data.
- Because real rates partly "recoupled" in 2026, a rule using real-rate direction might look good in-sample on 2026. Baur et al. found that fundamental rules failed SPA over 1990–2015, so this should not be trusted without long-history testing.
