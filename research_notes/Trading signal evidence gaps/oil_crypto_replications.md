## US oil: does intraday momentum in crude (first half-hour → last half-hour, and the EIA-day variant) hold up after 2018 and in 2025–2026? What about crude-only trend following and CTA energy results in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
I found no independent, after-cost, out-of-sample replication of the USO first-half-hour rule on CL futures or on 2019–2026 data. The best follow-up evidence comes from the same author group. It shows that market shocks wipe out the first-half-hour signal, and that on EIA days the predictive half-hour moves to the release window. Single-commodity crude trend has weak academic support, and 2025 was a poor year for CTAs, with energy among the losing sectors.

### Cited Findings
- **Original result.** Wen, Gong, Ma & Xu (2021, *Economic Modelling* 95:374–384) used one-minute USO data for 2006–2018. They report that the first half-hour return predicts the last half-hour return and that a timing strategy built on it earns "substantial profits" ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecmode/v95y2021icp374-384.html); [ScienceDirect abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0264999319310417)).
- **Conflicting abstracts.** Summaries disagree on whether the second-to-last half-hour also predicts the close. One says only the first half-hour does. Another abstract says "only the first half-hour and the second-to-last half-hour" do ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339877949_Intraday_momentum_and_return_predictability_Evidence_from_the_crude_oil_market); [Harbourfront summary](https://harbourfronts.com/does-intraday-momentum-exist-in-the-crude-oil-market/)). The primary paper needs checking.
- **EIA-day variant.** Indriawan, Lien, Wen & Xu use the EIA inventory release at 10:30 ET each Wednesday, which falls at the start of the third half-hour. On EIA days, the third half-hour predicts the last half-hour. On other days, only the first half-hour does ([SSRN 3822093](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3822093)). Note the overlap: the same authors (Wen, Xu) wrote this paper, so it is not independent of the original.
- **Fragility under shocks.** A 2023 *Energy Economics* paper extends USO data through 2022 and finds that "most market shocks obliterate the predictive power of first half-hour returns." Extreme shocks dominate intraday predictability ([ScienceDirect S0140988323006199](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0140988323006199)). This is the closest evidence after 2018, and it argues against using the rule unconditionally in event-heavy regimes.
- **Reversal in another crude market.** In Chinese (INE) crude oil futures, a study finds a strong intraday *reversal*, not momentum. The previous night's return predicts the day's return both in and out of sample ([search result summarising MPRA/ZHAW/Reading working papers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wly:jfutmk:v:42:y:2022:i:12:p:2218-2234)). I could not confirm which of the listed papers is the INE study, so treat the attribution as tentative.
- **Mixed out-of-sample record elsewhere.** In US equities, intraday-momentum predictability "disappears in the out-of-sample period." A 16-country study instead finds it significant in and out of sample in most countries ([MPRA 94445](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/94445/1/MPRA_paper_94445.pdf); [Notre Dame intramom](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). A multi-asset study covering 21 commodity futures (4 energy) from Dec 1974 to May 2020 reports intraday momentum across asset classes. Its energy-specific numbers were not visible in the snippet ([Nottingham repository](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/5273196)).
- **Practitioner replication (vendor, conflicting numbers).** QuantifiedStrategies re-tests the rule on **USO, not CL**, because "they are not trading WTI futures." One post reports a **1.85% annualized** return. Another page lists "Intraday Momentum Trading Strategy (19.6% Annual Returns)." Period and costs are unclear, and the two numbers cannot be reconciled ([X article](https://x.com/QuantifiedStrat/article/2032452456496443687); [QuantifiedStrategies crude page](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/crude-oil-futures/)). This is vendor content, not an independent academic replication.
- **Crude-only time-series momentum.** A 2024 *Pacific-Basin Finance Journal* paper finds "no supportive evidence" of time-series momentum across about 55 developed commodity futures markets, and none in the representative Asian crude market. Momentum also "does not significantly outperform" a naive strategy with no predictability ([ScienceDirect S0927538X24002245](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X24002245)). The sample period was not visible in the snippet.
- **CTA 2025, from SG Trend Index monthly reports via Top Traders Unplugged:**
  - January: +0.15%. Energy helped early, then crude fell more than 2% later in the month.
  - February: −3.19% MTD, as "crude oil lost ground as geopolitical catalysts fizzled."
  - April: −4.89%, taking YTD to −9.33%.
  - August: −7.56% YTD, with "reversals in energy" offsetting gains elsewhere.
  - November: +1.37%. December: +1.92%. The recovery began in September.

  Sources: [Jan 2025](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-january-2025/), [Feb 2025](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-february-2025/), [Apr 2025](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-april-2025/), [Aug 2025](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-august-2025/), [Nov 2025](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-november-2025/), [Dec 2025](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-december-2025/).
- **Sector view of 2025.** A Substack commentator says that in 2025 "gold and silver made huge gains while energy and agricultural contracts had made losses" for trend followers ([Rupak Ghose](https://rupakghose.substack.com/p/moving-beyond-fixed-income)). This is opinion, not index attribution.
- **2026 data exists but was not read.** A May 2026 SG Trend report exists ([TTU May 2026](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/trend-following-performance-report-may-2026/)). Its contents were not visible in search results.

### Inferences
- The oil intraday rule has never been independently replicated after costs on 2019–2026 data, as far as these searches show. Its 2006–2018 evidence is gross of costs and comes from one author group.
- On a CFD with a spread of a few cents, a 30-minute hold earning around 1.85% a year gross (the vendor's figure) would likely be wiped out by costs.
- Because shocks erase the signal, any test should split results by regime (2020 COVID, 2022 war, and 2025–2026 OPEC and geopolitical episodes) and use the real EIA calendar, including holiday shifts.
- No study reports a full sign reversal of the US crude rule. The only reversal evidence comes from Chinese crude futures.
- Crude-alone trend following is weaker than the portfolio-level commodity-trend evidence in the earlier report suggests. Single-market TSMOM in crude is statistically indistinguishable from a naive benchmark in at least one 2024 study. And 2025 was a losing year for energy within CTA portfolios. A crude-only 12-month or Donchian rule should be expected to have long flat or losing stretches.

### Gaps
- No full-year 2025 SG Trend Index total or energy-sector attribution was found. The H1 2026 energy contribution is also unknown.
- No 2025–2026 out-of-sample test of Donchian/turtle or the 1/3/12-month blend on WTI alone was found.
- No out-of-sample test of the Xu EIA-day variant after 2021 was found, and nothing independent of the Wen/Xu author group.
- The full texts of the Wen et al. paper and the 2023 tail-risk paper could not be fetched, so exact Sharpe ratios and cost breakevens are missing.

## Bitcoin/ETH: independent replications of the 50-day SMA rule, MAX-N next-day hold, the Zarattini Donchian ensemble and the 21–23 UTC seasonality, including 2025–2026 out-of-sample and after-fee results; single coin vs basket

### Takeaway
None of the four rules has a clean independent 2025–2026 out-of-sample test after fees in the sources I could reach. Independent academic work on Bitcoin moving-average rules is split. Detzel et al. find out-of-sample predictability. Hudson & Urquhart and a 2024 *International Review of Economics & Finance* (IREF) study find no out-of-sample outperformance for Bitcoin specifically, though some altcoins show it. Zarattini's after-fee Sharpe above 1.5 belongs to a **20-coin rotational basket** and is not a Bitcoin-only figure. Quantpedia's 21–23 UTC evidence stops at June 2023.

### Cited Findings
- **(a) Moving-average rules: positive side.** Detzel et al. find that price-to-moving-average ratios forecast daily Bitcoin returns in and out of sample, with "economically significant alpha and Sharpe ratio gains" over buy-and-hold ([ResearchGate: Bitcoin Predictability and Profitability via Technical Analysis](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323324259_Bitcoin_Predictability_and_Profitability_via_Technical_Analysis)).
- **(a) Moving-average rules: skeptical side.** Hudson & Urquhart test 5–100-day moving-average rules with breakeven costs. They find Bitcoin "does not offer any positive returns in the out-of-sample period," while other cryptocurrencies show predictability. They estimate Bitcoin transaction costs at about 50 bp (snippet) ([Annals of Operations Research 2021, 297:191–220](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10479-019-03357-1.pdf); [Reading repository](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/85715/8/Hudson-Urquhart2019_Article_TechnicalTradingAndCryptocurre.pdf)). I am not certain which of the two linked papers the cost figure comes from.
- **(a) Moving-average rules: later independent check.** "Are simple technical trading rules profitable in bitcoin markets?" (IREF 2024) "does not find out-of-sample (OOS) outperformance for bitcoin" ([ScienceDirect S1059056024003010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1059056024003010)).
- **(a) Moving-average rules: BTC, ETH and crypto indexes.** Le & Ruthbah (Aug 2024, Monash) tested simple moving-average rules on BTC, ETH and S&P crypto indexes. Shorter lookbacks give high Sharpe ratios, but "transaction costs may decrease profits due to frequent portfolio rebalancing" ([Monash CFS](https://www.monash.edu/business/monash-centre-for-financial-studies/our-research/all-projects/investment-strategy/trend-following-strategies-for-crypto-investors)).
- **(a) Grayscale (vendor).** Grayscale's momentum-signal report was last updated 8/9/2023 and does not cover 2024–2026 ([Grayscale](https://research.grayscale.com/reports/the-trend-is-your-friend-managing-bitcoins-volatility-with-momentum-signals)).
- **(b) MAX-N.** Quantpedia's own revisit is the only out-of-sample evidence found, covering Feb 2022 to Aug 2024. The search snippet says MIN "is not performing as well out of sample" while the MAX rule "remains very effective." No fee adjustment was visible ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/revisiting-trend-following-and-mean-reversion-strategies-in-bitcoin/)). I found no independent replication and no 2025–2026 update.
- **(c) Zarattini, Pagani & Barbon (Apr 2025), "Catching Crypto Trends."**
  - Universe: coins listed at least a year since 2015, with a 30-day median volume of at least $2M.
  - The headline is a survivorship-bias-free **rotational top-20 portfolio** from Jan 2015 to Mar 2025: Sharpe above 1.5 and 10.8% annual alpha versus BTC, net of fees.
  - Bitcoin is tested first on its own. Its standalone figures were not visible.

  Sources: [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2580.html), [Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/catching-crypto-trends-a-tactical-approach-for-bitcoin-and-altcoins/), [Barbon](https://abarbon.com/papers/catching-crypto-trends).
- **(c) Independent coverage of Zarattini.** CXO Advisory reviewed the paper, but its verdict is paywalled ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/crypto-asset-trend-following-strategies/)). The only other "replications" are hobbyist GitHub issues and PRs, which say their versions are "our own version; their exact rules were not verified" ([GitHub example](https://github.com/zebadee2kk/DeFi-TraderStack-Agent/issues/137); [another](https://github.com/cuauhtemocbe/btc-predictor/issues/181)). Neither counts as evidence.
- **(d) 21–23 UTC seasonality.** Padysak & Vojtko used hourly Gemini data from 9 Oct 2015 to 3 Feb 2022, and extended it to 30 Jun 2023. The strongest hours are 22:00 and 23:00 UTC, with Friday the best day ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/the-seasonality-of-bitcoin/); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/378110832_The_Seasonality_of_Bitcoin)). A Quantpedia extended-data analysis cites a 37.26% annualized return for holding BTC two hours a day (snippet, gross) ([Quantpedia overnight anomalies](https://quantpedia.com/are-there-seasonal-intraday-or-overnight-anomalies-in-bitcoin/)). I found no test after June 2023, and only marketing-style restatements such as TradingView scripts citing "33% annualized" ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/script/IzFZxayj/)).
- **(d) Related newer work.** Concretum/Zarattini studied weekly seasonality in a high-frequency BTC trend ensemble, comparing 2018 to 2020 H1 with 2020 H2 to 2025. They report that the patterns carry over into later hours and favor intraday trend. Performance numbers were not visible ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/seasonality-in-bitcoin-intraday-trend-trading/)).
- **2026 context: price indicators decaying.** Molnar (arXiv 2607.26188, July 2026; summarized by Quantpedia) argues that classic BTC cycle indicators (MVRV, Mayer Multiple, Pi Cycle) have stopped triggering as volatility shrinks. He proposes a "halving clock" instead. His own out-of-sample window is "a single 526-day span dominated by one bear market," which he calls "suggestive, not decisive" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26188); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/bitcoin-runs-on-a-clock-why-every-price-indicator-dies-and-the-halving-clock-doesnt/)). These are on-chain and cycle indicators, not the SMA, MAX or Donchian rules. A PR Newswire release promoting it is marketing ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/every-famous-bitcoin-top-indicator-went-silent-at-the-all-time-high-new-research-explains-why-302831905.html)).
- **Basket evidence (2026, not independent of the multi-asset approach).** AdaptiveTrend (arXiv 2602.11708) reports an out-of-sample Sharpe of 2.41 over 36 months (2022–2024) across more than 150 pairs. That makes it a basket result, not Bitcoin-only and not 2025–2026 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11708v1)).

### Inferences
- **Single coin vs basket.** The strongest after-fee crypto trend result (Zarattini) is a basket result. Independent Bitcoin-only studies (Hudson & Urquhart; IREF 2024) find weaker or no out-of-sample edge for BTC, while some altcoins show predictability. A BTC-only or ETH-only implementation should expect a materially lower Sharpe than the 1.5+ headline. The value likely comes partly from cross-coin rotation and volatility targeting.
- MAX-N and 21–23 UTC are high-turnover rules supported only by the original vendor/author out-of-sample tests, with no published fee-adjusted 2024–2026 updates. At the roughly 50 bp round-trip costs Hudson & Urquhart cite, or typical CFD spreads, the 2-hour seasonality trade is especially at risk.
- No source found shows any of the four rules reversing in 2025–2026. But the absence of published tests is not evidence that they persisted.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 out-of-sample, fee-adjusted test was found for any of the four rules. This needs an in-house walk-forward test from Jan 2024 or Apr 2025 onward.
- Zarattini's BTC-only figures and its results at 0.25%/0.50% fees were not readable (full text not fetched).
- The CXO verdict on Zarattini is paywalled.
- No Kaiko, Coinbase or BIS study on these specific timing rules was found in these searches.

## CFD overnight swap/financing costs for USOIL and BTCUSD (e.g. Exness) and their effect on multi-day trend rules

### Takeaway
At Exness, USOIL is widely reported as swap-free on standard accounts, which makes multi-day crude trend rules cheap to hold. BTCUSD financing is disputed: some sources say all crypto is swap-free, while a third-party table shows a long swap. Mainstream brokers such as IG charge roughly 15–23% a year on notional for long BTC CFDs, which would wipe out most trend-following edge over multi-week holds.

### Cited Findings
- **Exness USOIL.** Exness's own USOIL page says USOIL is swap-free under the Swap-Free program, and that "only USOIL" among its crude contracts qualifies ([Exness USOIL](https://www.exness.com/commodities/usoil/); [exn.fxsignup oil](https://exn.fxsignup.com/en/trade/oil.html)). An older Exness-branded issuu piece also says swaps were eliminated on "USOil" ([issuu](https://issuu.com/exness_blog/docs/exness.docx/s/88604137)). A third-party article says Exness cut fees on crude oil and bitcoin ([DayTrading.com](https://www.daytrading.com/exness-slashes-fees-on-crude-oil-and-bitcoin)).
- **Exness energies.** One blog says energies carry a single daily charge with no triple swap ([issuu](https://issuu.com/exness_india/docs/exness_india.docx/s/115102937)). That applies where a swap exists at all.
- **Exness BTCUSD: conflicting.**
  - Exness's crypto page says a **triple swap** applies to BTCUSD, ETHUSD, ETHBTC and BTCUSDT held over Friday night ([Exness crypto](https://www.exness.com/crypto/)). That only matters if a swap applies.
  - FX Recap says "all cryptocurrencies" are swap-free ([FX Recap](https://fxrecap.com/top-broker/exness-review/islamic-account)).
  - A third-party table lists a BTCUSD long swap of **−223 points (about $2.05)** (snippet; lot size and date unclear) ([exn.fxsignup swap](https://exn.fxsignup.com/en/trade/swap.html)).
  - Swap-free status applies only to the published instrument list, except for clients registered in Islamic countries ([MarketGB](https://marketgb.com/global/articles/ic-markets-or-exness-swap-free-account/)).
- **Exness timing.** Swaps are charged once a day, Monday to Friday, and rates can change daily ([Exness commodities](https://www.exness.com/uz/commodities/)). They are reported to be applied at 21:00 GMT in summer and 22:00 GMT in winter (blog) ([issuu](https://issuu.com/exnesstrading/docs/how_to_exness_trade/s/109168875)).
- **IG BTC/ETH CFDs.** Funding is a variable market component plus a **10% p.a. admin fee** on full notional. Older IG pages show a fixed 0.0417%/day (15% p.a.) plus a 0.02778%/day (10% p.a.) admin fee ([IG UK crypto CFD details](https://www.ig.com/uk/help-and-support/articles/681837-cryptocurrencies-cfd-product-details); [IG overnight funding](https://www.ig.com/en-ch/help-and-support/cfds/fees-and-charges/why-is-overnight-funding-charged-and-how-is-it-calculated-)).
- **Cross-broker survey.** Long BTC financing runs about 16% a year (360-day basis) at one broker and about 0.0616% a day (about 22.5% a year) at another. That is roughly $4.40–$6.20 a day per $10,000 long. Shorts cost about 4% a year at one broker ([Webopedia 2026](https://www.webopedia.com/crypto/exchanges/crypto-cfd-trading-platforms/)).
- **Perpetual-futures benchmark.** BTC perpetual funding averaged about 9.6% a year for longs in one aggregator snapshot, about 19 days old at search time ([DeFiRate](https://defirate.com/perp/bitcoin/)).

### Inferences
- **USOIL at Exness.** If USOIL is swap-free on the account type used, a 12-month or Donchian crude trend rule pays only the spread at entry and exit. But a swap-free CFD may also roll or adjust prices differently from futures, which a backtest on futures data would miss. Check this against the MT5 symbol specification.
- **BTC trend rules at a 15–23% a year financing broker.** A trend rule in the market about 50% of the time pays roughly 7–11% a year in financing. That is comparable to Zarattini's 10.8% alpha, so most of the edge would go to financing. The Donchian ensemble's up-to-2× leverage doubles this cost. Under a triple Friday swap, weekend carry costs three days.
- **Short-hold rules.** Rules that close intraday (21–23 UTC, the oil last half-hour) avoid swaps if they close before the 21:00/22:00 GMT rollover. The 21–23 UTC BTC window straddles the Exness rollover time, so a position held through 22:00 GMT in winter, or 21:00 GMT in summer, could incur a swap, and a triple one on Fridays, when Quantpedia says the effect is strongest. This needs checking.
- **Backtesting implication.** Model financing explicitly per night held, using the live Exness swap table, rather than assuming zero.

### Gaps
- Current exact Exness swap values for BTCUSD and ETHUSD (long and short, in points per lot), and whether they are swap-free on Standard or Pro accounts in the relevant region, could not be verified because exness.com was unreachable. Check the MT5 symbol specification or the Exness trading calculator.
- No source showed whether USOIL's swap-free status has conditions, such as a limit on the number of days held or an "extended swap-free" tier.
